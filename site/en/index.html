Can the 2027 timetable continue the progress Zurich's public transport made up to 2024?

From 2022 to 2024, Zurich became clearly better connected: in those two years, about 19 % more residents and 23 % more full-time jobs came within a 30-minute public transport trip. Since then the average has barely changed. Major construction projects are slowing down how well the city and the surrounding agglomeration are connected. Several projects and changes are due with the 2027 timetable, but will they substantially improve how well it is connected?

    How these maps are made

    The map is cut into 2,461 hexagons of the H3 grid (resolution 9, about 0.1 km² each). From the centre of each one, the same model as the map at the top computes the travel time to every 200 m cell, on each timetable year: walk to the nearest stations, wait, ride, change, walk on, or walk the whole way.

    A hexagon's colour is the number of residents or of full-time jobs within the chosen travel time. Residents and full-time jobs are those of each timetable year (STATPOP 2022 for the 2022 timetable, 2024 for the 2024 timetable, 2025 for the 2026–2027 timetables; STATENT 2022 for the 2022 timetable, 2024 for the 2024–2027 timetables), so a change between two years combines the network with people moving and jobs appearing or going.

    These maps use every line in the timetable, city buses, regional buses and lake boats included, as with « Bus and boat » ticked on the map. Inside the city the buses matter most: from the median hexagon in the twelve Kreise they add 38 % more residents within 30 minutes (2027).

    The colour classes stay the same across years, so two years can be compared by eye. The change map counts small differences as « about the same ».

    Why the counts change from year to year

    Across the whole map, almost all the gain came between 2022 and 2024 (+19.1 % residents and +23.0 % full-time jobs within 30 min of the average starting point, most of it the network: the map's residents grew 2.4 %); after that the average barely moves (+0.7 % 2024→2026, −0.4 % 2026→2027). That period includes the Limmattalbahn's second stage (December 2022). Since then gains and losses roughly cancel out: by 2026, 17 % of places reach over 5 % more residents and 11 % over 5 % fewer. The 2026 timetable has the SZU construction and tram diversions described below, and 2027 is a draft with fewer trips than the final timetable, so the plateau is not yet a decline.

    Residents and full-time jobs are those of each timetable year, so a change in either combines the network with where people live and work; set the full-time jobs against the region's total in the last column. Where two years share a grid (full-time jobs from 2024, residents from 2026), the change is the network alone.

    For scale, "Full-time jobs in the region" is the actual STATENT count (full-time equivalents) for the map's municipalities in each year, or the latest published year where none exists yet (STATENT 2024 is the newest): the region went from 577,033 full-time jobs in 2022 to 596,632 in 2024, +19,599.

    2024 → 2026: residents within 30 min go from 429,785 to 422,192 (−7,593), full-time jobs from 488,470 to 485,487. The SZU lines S4 and S10 no longer call at Zürich HB SZU but start at Selnau (works on the HB–Selnau section), so the Sihltal and the S10 stops in town need an extra change from HB, and Adliswil, Leimbach, Manegg, Sood-Oberleimbach and Triemli slip past 30 minutes; trams also run construction diversions (temporary lines 50 and 51). The core got faster at the same time: residents within 15 min went from 78,939 to 81,811 (+3.6 %, while the map's residents grew 0.9 %).

    Each year is one reference weekday of the published timetable, 7:00–20:00, with no delays or disruptions, so a diversion running on that day shows up in that year's figures.
