What these resident and full-time job counts mean

Both move with the timetable year. Residents are the BFS STATPOP grid (STATPOP 2022 for the 2022 timetable, 2024 for the 2024 timetable, 2025 for the 2026–2027 timetables; permanent residents, by home address); full-time jobs the STATENT grid (STATENT 2022 for the 2022 timetable, 2024 for the 2024–2027 timetables; full-time equivalents of all sectors, counted where people work). The map covers the city of Zurich and neighbouring municipalities: 723k residents and 601k full-time jobs in all on the 2027 timetable, more than the city alone.

Full-time jobs are full-time equivalents: two half-time posts count as one. Staff are counted at the workplace their employer registers, which for firms with several sites is not always where they actually sit.

The BFS rounds small hectare counts for privacy, and each 100 m hectare goes to the 200 m map cell its centre falls in, so figures for a few streets are rough; totals over a whole isochrone hold up much better.

Switching timetable years changes how far the network reaches and also where people live and work. Where two timetables share a grid (full-time jobs from 2024 on, residents from 2026 on), the difference between them is the network alone.

Questions

How the times are computed, and where the data comes from.
How long does it take to cross Zurich by public transport?

From Zürich HB, 33% of the 288 tram and rail stops on the map are less than 15 minutes away and 87% less than 30 minutes, walking and waiting included. The farthest, Felsenegg, takes about 51 minutes.

Where do the timetables come from?

From the official Swiss timetable published by opentransportdata.swiss (GTFS), clipped to the Zurich area, downloaded on 10 November 2026 and valid until 11 December 2027. Times are those of Tuesday 23 November 2027, between 7:00 and 20:00.

Are buses included?

Yes, as an option: tick « Bus and boat » in the map's settings. By default only trams, S-Bahn and trains, funiculars and the Adliswil–Felsenegg cable car are used. Waits at rarely served stops are capped at 15 minutes.

How are travel times computed?

Walk to the stop at 4.5 km/h in a straight line, wait half the interval between two departures, ride for the scheduled time between stops, change with 1.5 minutes of walking, and allow 1.5 minutes to reach a railway platform. On a section that several lines share (HB to Hardbrücke, tram trunks) the rider takes whichever comes first and changes where the lines split; a stop served by one line waits for that line. No real-time data or disruptions: this is the city « on paper ».

Do hills and the lake count?

Yes. Walking is slower uphill and a little faster on gentle descents (Tobler's hiking function on swisstopo elevation), and no walk crosses the Zürichsee, Greifensee or Türlersee. Rivers count as crossable, since bridges are close together in town.

How are bike times computed?

On the streets open to bicycles in OpenStreetMap, one-way streets included where cycling against the traffic is allowed: 20 km/h on the flat, about 10 km/h on a 5 % climb, 25 km/h on a 3 % descent and 30 km/h on 5 % or steeper, plus a minute to unlock and a minute to park. The street network comes from the GIS_playground_ZH cycling project.

Where do the resident and full-time job figures come from?

From the Federal Statistical Office's hectare grids, each timetable year with its own: STATPOP 2022 for the 2022 timetable, 2024 for the 2024 timetable, 2025 for the 2026–2027 timetables (permanent residents) and STATENT 2022 for the 2022 timetable, 2024 for the 2024–2027 timetables (full-time equivalents, all sectors; newer grids are not published yet). Each 100 m square is added to the 200 m map cell it falls in; small counts are rounded by the BFS for privacy, so totals are approximate. « Within reach » sums the cells reached within each travel time.

Why do some tram lines look unusual?

The timetable is the published one for the reference day, construction diversions included (lines 50 and 51 in 2026, for example).

Data sources
Timetables (GTFS)
Swiss national timetable, published by opentransportdata.swiss (terms of use); older years from the Mobility Database archive. Clipped to the Zurich area.
  • Swiss timetable 2022, last version archived by the Mobility Database (mdb-1092), valid 2021-12-12 → 2022-12-14, downloaded 5 October 2026.
  • Swiss timetable 2024, last version archived by the Mobility Database (mdb-2144), valid 2023-12-10 → 2024-12-19, downloaded 5 October 2026.
  • Swiss timetable 2026 (opentransportdata.swiss), valid 2025-12-14 → 2026-12-12, downloaded 5 October 2026.
  • Swiss timetable 2027, pre-season version (opentransportdata.swiss), valid 2026-12-13 → 2027-12-11, downloaded 10 November 2026.
Municipal and district boundaries
swissBOUNDARIES3D (swisstopo, via api3.geo.admin.ch) and the 12 Stadtkreise from Stadt Zürich Open Data, downloaded 5 October 2026. Open government data.
Residents
STATPOP 2022 for the 2022 timetable, 2024 for the 2024 timetable, 2025 for the 2026–2027 timetables hectare grids, Federal Statistical Office (BFS) via data.geo.admin.ch, downloaded 10 October 2026.
Full-time jobs
STATENT 2022 for the 2022 timetable, 2024 for the 2024–2027 timetables hectare grids (full-time equivalents, all sectors), BFS via data.geo.admin.ch, downloaded 7 October 2026.
Full-time jobs in the region
BFS STAT-TAB table px-x-0602010000_102 (STATENT full-time equivalents by municipality and year), summed over the map's municipalities, downloaded 10 October 2026.
Elevation
swisstopo terrain model (swissALTI3D / DHM25) sampled every 100 m through the geo.admin.ch profile service, downloaded 5 October 2026.
Lines, lakes, parks and cycling streets
© OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL), via the Overpass API, downloaded 5 October 2026.
Base map
Faint street layer drawn from the same OpenStreetMap network as the bike layer (major roads, streets and paths; service roads left out), © OpenStreetMap contributors. Untick « Streets » to hide it.
Bus lines
Drawn from OpenStreetMap bus and trolleybus route relations, matched to the timetable by line number (the Swiss GTFS has no line shapes); shown with « Bus and boat ».
Hexagons
H3 grid (Apache 2.0), resolution 9, via the h3 Python library; reach computed by build_h3.py from the same timetables.
Method
Fork of À portée de tram by Camille Roux (MIT). Full provenance (URLs, SHA-256, dates) in sources/.
