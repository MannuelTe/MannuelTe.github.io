Map Everywhere Over time Questions DE

Can the 2027 timetable continue the progress Zurich's public transport made up to 2024?

From 2022 to 2024, Zurich became clearly better connected: in those two years, about 19 % more residents and 23 % more full-time jobs came within a 30-minute public transport trip. Since then the average has barely changed. Major construction projects are slowing down how well the city and the surrounding agglomeration are connected. Several projects and changes are due with the 2027 timetable, but will they substantially improve how well it is connected?

The map below colours Zurich by travel time from Zürich HB, walking and waiting included: switch between 2022, 2024, 2026 and 2027, drag the start anywhere or click to set a destination.

    How these maps are made

    The map is cut into 2,461 hexagons of the H3 grid (resolution 9, about 0.1 km² each). From the centre of each one, the same model as the map at the top computes the travel time to every 200 m cell, on each timetable year: walk to the nearest stations, wait, ride, change, walk on, or walk the whole way.

    A hexagon's colour is the number of residents or of full-time jobs within the chosen travel time. Residents and full-time jobs are those of each timetable year (STATPOP 2022 for the 2022 timetable, 2024 for the 2024 timetable, 2025 for the 2026–2027 timetables; STATENT 2022 for the 2022 timetable, 2024 for the 2024–2027 timetables), so a change between two years combines the network with people moving and jobs appearing or going.

    These maps use every line in the timetable, city buses, regional buses and lake boats included, as with « Bus and boat » ticked on the map. Inside the city the buses matter most: from the median hexagon in the twelve Kreise they add 38 % more residents within 30 minutes (2027).

    The colour classes stay the same across years, so two years can be compared by eye. The change map counts small differences as « about the same ».

    Why the counts change from year to year

    Across the whole map, almost all the gain came between 2022 and 2024 (+19.1 % residents and +23.0 % full-time jobs within 30 min of the average starting point, most of it the network: the map's residents grew 2.4 %); after that the average barely moves (+0.7 % 2024→2026, −0.4 % 2026→2027). That period includes the Limmattalbahn's second stage (December 2022). Since then gains and losses roughly cancel out: by 2026, 17 % of places reach over 5 % more residents and 11 % over 5 % fewer. The 2026 timetable has the SZU construction and tram diversions described below, and 2027 is a draft with fewer trips than the final timetable, so the plateau is not yet a decline.

    Residents and full-time jobs are those of each timetable year, so a change in either combines the network with where people live and work; set the full-time jobs against the region's total in the last column. Where two years share a grid (full-time jobs from 2024, residents from 2026), the change is the network alone.

    For scale, "Full-time jobs in the region" is the actual STATENT count (full-time equivalents) for the map's municipalities in each year, or the latest published year where none exists yet (STATENT 2024 is the newest): the region went from 577,033 full-time jobs in 2022 to 596,632 in 2024, +19,599.

    2024 → 2026: residents within 30 min go from 429,785 to 422,192 (−7,593), full-time jobs from 488,470 to 485,487. The SZU lines S4 and S10 no longer call at Zürich HB SZU but start at Selnau (works on the HB–Selnau section), so the Sihltal and the S10 stops in town need an extra change from HB, and Adliswil, Leimbach, Manegg, Sood-Oberleimbach and Triemli slip past 30 minutes; trams also run construction diversions (temporary lines 50 and 51). The core got faster at the same time: residents within 15 min went from 78,939 to 81,811 (+3.6 %, while the map's residents grew 0.9 %).

    Each year is one reference weekday of the published timetable, 7:00–20:00, with no delays or disruptions, so a diversion running on that day shows up in that year's figures.

    What these resident and full-time job counts mean

    Both move with the timetable year. Residents are the BFS STATPOP grid (STATPOP 2022 for the 2022 timetable, 2024 for the 2024 timetable, 2025 for the 2026–2027 timetables; permanent residents, by home address); full-time jobs the STATENT grid (STATENT 2022 for the 2022 timetable, 2024 for the 2024–2027 timetables; full-time equivalents of all sectors, counted where people work). The map covers the city of Zurich and neighbouring municipalities: 723k residents and 601k full-time jobs in all on the 2027 timetable, more than the city alone.

    Full-time jobs are full-time equivalents: two half-time posts count as one. Staff are counted at the workplace their employer registers, which for firms with several sites is not always where they actually sit.

    The BFS rounds small hectare counts for privacy, and each 100 m hectare goes to the 200 m map cell its centre falls in, so figures for a few streets are rough; totals over a whole isochrone hold up much better.

    Switching timetable years changes how far the network reaches and also where people live and work. Where two timetables share a grid (full-time jobs from 2024 on, residents from 2026 on), the difference between them is the network alone.

    Questions

    How the times are computed, and where the data comes from.
    How long does it take to cross Zurich by public transport?

    From Zürich HB, 33% of the 288 tram and rail stops on the map are less than 15 minutes away and 87% less than 30 minutes, walking and waiting included. The farthest, Felsenegg, takes about 51 minutes.

    Where do the timetables come from?

    From the official Swiss timetable published by opentransportdata.swiss (GTFS), clipped to the Zurich area, downloaded on 10 November 2026 and valid until 11 December 2027. Times are those of Tuesday 23 November 2027, between 7:00 and 20:00.

    Are buses included?

    Yes, as an option: tick « Bus and boat » under the map. By default only trams, S-Bahn and trains, funiculars and the Adliswil–Felsenegg cable car are used. Waits at rarely served stops are capped at 15 minutes.

    How are travel times computed?

    Walk to the stop at 4.5 km/h in a straight line, wait half the interval between two departures, ride for the scheduled time between stops, change with 1.5 minutes of walking, and allow 1.5 minutes to reach a railway platform. On a section that several lines share (HB to Hardbrücke, tram trunks) the rider takes whichever comes first and changes where the lines split; a stop served by one line waits for that line. No real-time data or disruptions: this is the city « on paper ».

    Do hills and the lake count?

    Yes. Walking is slower uphill and a little faster on gentle descents (Tobler's hiking function on swisstopo elevation), and no walk crosses the Zürichsee, Greifensee or Türlersee. Rivers count as crossable, since bridges are close together in town.

    How are bike times computed?

    On the streets open to bicycles in OpenStreetMap, one-way streets included where cycling against the traffic is allowed: 20 km/h on the flat, about 10 km/h on a 5 % climb, 25 km/h on a 3 % descent and 30 km/h on 5 % or steeper, plus a minute to unlock and a minute to park. The street network comes from the GIS_playground_ZH cycling project.

    Where do the resident and full-time job figures come from?

    From the Federal Statistical Office's hectare grids, each timetable year with its own: STATPOP 2022 for the 2022 timetable, 2024 for the 2024 timetable, 2025 for the 2026–2027 timetables (permanent residents) and STATENT 2022 for the 2022 timetable, 2024 for the 2024–2027 timetables (full-time equivalents, all sectors; newer grids are not published yet). Each 100 m square is added to the 200 m map cell it falls in; small counts are rounded by the BFS for privacy, so totals are approximate. The table under the map sums the cells reached within each isochrone.

    Why do some tram lines look unusual?

    The timetable is the published one for the reference day, construction diversions included (lines 50 and 51 in 2026, for example).
